Background: I recently defended my doctoral dissertation in Biomedical Engineering at Penn State, working in Dr. Sri-Rajasekhar Kothapalli's lab. My research focused on developing novel approaches for combining ultrasound and optical imaging modalities to study the neurovascular basis of ultrasound neuromodulation and functional ultrasound imaging. For the former, I developed transparent ultrasound transducer (TUT) platforms for simultaneous ultrasound stimulation and multimodal optical neuroimaging. For the latter, I worked toward combining functional ultrasound with optical imaging to derive multiparametric neuronal and hemodynamic information in the rodent brain. My work bridges device fabrication, multimodal imaging system development, cross-validation of fUS imaging signals, and in vivo neuroscience applications, with an emphasis on understanding ultrasound brain stimulation, neurovascular coupling, and cerebrovascular function.

I am a Cross-Disciplinary Neural Engineering (CDNE) T32 Fellow funded by the NIH (T32NS115667) and a Freiburg Rising Star Fellow from the University of Freiburg. I have been recognized with multiple awards, including the Gordon Research Conference Best Poster Award (2024), the Society for Neuroscience Trainee Professional Development Award, and the John C. and Joanne H. Villforth Graduate Scholarship. Prior to Penn State, I completed an M.S. (Research) in Photonics at the Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur and a B.E. in Electronics and Communications Engineering at the University of Delhi.

I work at the intersection of engineering and neuroscience, driven by the belief that deciphering brain function is both a profound scientific challenge and an urgent clinical need. I find great satisfaction in leveraging my engineering background to build novel imaging and therapeutic tools, and in combining existing approaches to expand the scale and richness of information we can capture. Above all, I am motivated by the science itself: understanding how the brain works, and what goes wrong in neurological dysfunction.
